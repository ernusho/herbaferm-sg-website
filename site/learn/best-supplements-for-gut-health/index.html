Supplements

Best supplements for gut health: how to choose, and what the evidence says

There is no single best supplement. There are better questions to ask. This guide gives you a checklist.

By Herbaferm editorial teamMedical reviewer: PendingPublished 2 Nov 2026 (draft)

In short
  • 'Gut health supplement' covers very different things: probiotics, prebiotics, postbiotics, fibre and fermented products.[1][2][3]
  • Evidence is specific to the strain or ingredient and the condition studied. A product that helps one group may do nothing for another.[4]
  • Compare brands on clarity of ingredients, evidence for that exact product, testing and honest claims, not on star ratings or superlatives.

What counts as a gut health supplement?

The label covers several different categories. Scientists have agreed definitions for most of them:

TypePlain meaning
ProbioticLive microorganisms that, given in adequate amounts, may give a health benefit to the person taking them[1]
PrebioticA substrate that microorganisms already in the host use selectively, giving a health benefit[2]
PostbioticA preparation of inanimate (not living) microorganisms and/or their components[3]
Dietary fibrePlant carbohydrates that are not digested in the small intestine; higher intakes are associated with better health outcomes in large reviews[5]

Which supplements for gut health have the best evidence?

It depends on who you are and what the question is. The American Gastroenterological Association reviewed probiotics for a range of digestive conditions in 2020 and recommended them for only a few specific situations, while advising against routine use for others. The evidence was often of low quality and specific to particular strains.[4] In other words, "probiotic" is not one treatment. The same caution applies to every other category on the label.

How do I compare gut health supplements?

  1. Is the type clear? A label should say whether it contains live organisms, fibre, fermented extracts or something else.
  2. Is there evidence for this exact product? Look for studies on the same ingredient or strain at a similar dose, not just on the category.
  3. Is it tested? Ask for a certificate of analysis or batch testing.
  4. Are the claims modest and accurate? In Singapore, health supplements are not pre-approved by the Health Sciences Authority, so "HSA approved" should be a red flag.[6] Be wary of words like "cure", "detox" or "treats".
  5. Does it fit your routine? Storage, taste, cost per day and whether you will actually take it for weeks.

Do food and routine matter more than supplements?

Long-term diet shapes the gut microbiome, and a short change in diet can shift it within days.[7][8] Plant variety, whole grains, legumes, vegetables and fermented foods are the usual starting points. A supplement is an add-on, not a replacement. See our guides on how to improve gut health and fermented foods.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best supplement for gut health?

There is no single best supplement. Evidence depends on the specific ingredient or strain and on the person. Compare products on clear labelling, evidence for that exact product, testing and honest claims.

Are gut health supplements approved by the HSA in Singapore?

No. Singapore does not pre-approve health supplements, so no supplement can be described as HSA approved.

Should I take a supplement or change my diet first?

Diet is the usual starting point: plant variety, fibre and fermented foods. A supplement is an add-on, and anyone with persistent symptoms should see a doctor.
