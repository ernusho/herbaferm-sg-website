Microbiome

Microbial dysbiosis and rebiosis: what they mean and how the gut community recovers

An explainer based on the Herbaferm founder's book The Holistic Microbiome, set beside what independent research says.

Based on The Holistic Microbiome by Adam Bóday, founder of Herbaferm HungaryPage drafted by the Herbaferm editorial teamReview by Adam Bóday: Pending confirmationMedical reviewer: PendingPublished 2 Nov 2026 (draft)

In short
  • Dysbiosis means an imbalance in the microbial community of the body, usually the gut. It is a description, not a diagnosis.[1]
  • Researchers link dysbiosis with many conditions, but whether it is a cause or a result is still debated in most cases.[2]
  • Rebiosis is a term used by Herbaferm's founder for the restoration of a healthy host-microbe relationship; it is not a medical treatment and is not an established clinical term.[3]

What is microbial dysbiosis?

Dysbiosis is an imbalance of the microbial community of the body, most often the gut. The changes can involve fewer types of microbes, a shift in which groups dominate, or a change in what the community does.[1] The word describes a pattern, and researchers use it in different ways, so on its own it is not a diagnosis.[4]

What causes dysbiosis?

Reviews list several triggers: antibiotics and other medicines, infections, changes in diet, and ageing.[1] In a study of repeated antibiotic courses, the gut community did not fully return to its starting state in the weeks that followed.[5] Diet can also shift the community within days.[6] The founder's book and Herbaferm's materials add a wider argument that modern life, industrial food, treated water and chemical exposure are contributing factors. That argument is Herbaferm's perspective, and the sources cited here do not establish it.[7]

How is dysbiosis linked to health?

Studies have found altered gut microbial communities in people with a wide range of conditions, including inflammatory bowel disease, obesity and type 2 diabetes. In most cases it remains unclear whether the change helps cause the condition, results from it, or both.[2][4] Herbaferm does not claim that any product treats, prevents or reverses any of these conditions.

What is rebiosis?

Rebiosis is a term used by Herbaferm's founder, Adam Bóday, for an eco-physiological process of regeneration, rebalance or re-establishment of the symbiosis between the human host and its microbes after a disturbed state.[7][3] It is not a standard clinical term. The word has also appeared in the research literature, for example in a review of metaproteomics for monitoring the human microbiome.[8] We use it here to describe the idea of recovery, not to promise an outcome.

What does The Holistic Microbiome say about dysbiosis and rebiosis?

The book is a 2026 textbook and methodological guide by Adam Bóday and Ábel Bóday. Its sixth chapter is titled "Eubiosis, dysbiosis, rebiosis: dynamic equilibrium and regeneration", with sections on a functioning ecology, patterns of dysbiosis and "natural regeneration and clear-cutting-free stewardship".[9] We read that last title as a forestry-style image: support the ecosystem so it can regrow, rather than wipe it out and start again. We have only seen the book's opening chapter and contents list, so this page summarises its framing, not the chapter's detail.

The author's note says the book is "a synthesis of currently available literature on the microbiome and a decade of dedicated research", not "a collection of absolute truths or dogmatic assertions", and that it is not a substitute for medical advice.[9] We have tried to keep to that spirit.

What does the founder say supports recovery?

In his writing and talks, Bóday argues that a recovering community needs diverse natural microbial input, unprocessed plant foods, activity and rest, and that the aim is to supply the natural diversity that is missing from modern diets and water rather than to find a single "best" microbe.[7][9] This overlaps with general dietary advice: a variety of plant foods, fibre and fermented foods.[10][11] Where these ideas go beyond published evidence, we say so.

Frequently asked questions

What is microbial dysbiosis?

An imbalance in the microbial community of the body, usually the gut. It is a description of a pattern, not a diagnosis.

What is rebiosis?

A term used by Herbaferm's founder, Adam Bóday, for the restoration of a healthy symbiosis between the host and its microbes. It is not a standard clinical term and is not a treatment.

Can dysbiosis be tested?

Microbiome tests can describe which microbes are present, but there is no agreed clinical test for dysbiosis, and consumer tests can vary between providers.
