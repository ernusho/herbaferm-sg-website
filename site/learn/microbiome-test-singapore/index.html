Microbiome

Microbiome test in Singapore: what it measures and what it cannot tell you

A stool-based gut microbiome test counts which microbes are present. It does not diagnose anything. Here is how to read one.

By Herbaferm editorial teamMedical reviewer: PendingPublished 2 Nov 2026 (draft)

In short
  • A microbiome test reads microbial DNA from a small stool sample and reports which bacteria are present and in what proportions.[1]
  • It is not a medical diagnosis. Independent testing found large differences between commercial services given the same sample.[2]
  • Use a report as a conversation starter with a dietitian or doctor, not as a reason to start or stop a treatment.

What is a microbiome test?

A gut microbiome test looks at the community of microbes in your digestive tract. You collect a small stool sample at home and post it to a laboratory. The lab extracts bacterial DNA, sequences it and compares the result with reference databases.[1] The report typically lists the types of bacteria found, their relative amounts and a diversity score.

Who offers a microbiome test in Singapore?

Singapore has a home-grown option. AMILI, a Singapore start-up founded in 2019, built an Asian gut microbiome database from stool samples of healthy local residents and launched what it described in 2021 as South East Asia's first gut microbiome test with personalised nutrition recommendations.[1][3] AMILI's own website (amili.asia) describes its current services. Overseas kits can also be posted to Singapore. Herbaferm does not sell or endorse any test, and this page is not a review of one.

What does a microbiome report actually show?

Part of the reportWhat it tells youWhat it does not tell you
Bacterial types and proportionsWhich groups were detected in that one sampleWhether your mix is "good" or "bad": researchers are still working out what a healthy mix looks like[4]
Diversity scoreHow varied the detected community isA single number cannot show whether a symptom has a microbial cause
Food suggestionsGeneral, diet-based ideas such as eating more plant fibreThat a particular food will change your results; diet does shift the microbiome, but individual responses vary[5]

How reliable is a consumer microbiome test?

This is the main caution. In a 2024 preprint, researchers sent the same standardised human stool material to seven commercial direct-to-consumer services. The results differed widely within and between companies, by roughly as much as the biological differences between donors, and the authors called for quality standards and regulatory oversight.[2] A preprint has not completed peer review, but it is a useful reminder that two tests on the same sample can read differently.

Herbaferm founder Adam Bóday's textbook The Holistic Microbiome includes a supplementary chapter on interpreting stool microbiome reports. Its section titles flag the same issues: relative abundance versus absolute quantity (the "snapshot fallacy"), correlation versus causation, reproducibility, and the need for standardisation.[6]

When is a microbiome test worth considering?

If you are curious, a test can be a starting point for a discussion about food and routine. If you have persistent digestive symptoms, blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss or pain, see a doctor first: a stool microbiome report is not the right tool to investigate these.

Frequently asked questions

Does a microbiome test diagnose a condition?

No. It describes which microbes were detected in a stool sample. It cannot diagnose a disease, and independent testing shows that results can vary between commercial services.

How is a gut microbiome test done?

You collect a small stool sample at home, post it to a laboratory, and the lab sequences the microbial DNA and compiles a report.

Is there a microbiome test made in Singapore?

Yes. AMILI, a Singapore company, launched a stool-based test in 2021. Herbaferm is not affiliated with AMILI and does not endorse any test.
