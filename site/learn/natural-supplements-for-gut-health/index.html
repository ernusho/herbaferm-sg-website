Natural supplements for gut health: fibre, fermented and herbal options explained

'Natural' is a marketing word, not a safety guarantee. Here is what the main natural options are and how to read the label.

By Herbaferm editorial teamMedical reviewer: PendingPublished 2 Nov 2026 (draft)

In short
  • 'Natural' has no fixed legal meaning on a supplement label. Natural does not mean safe or proven.
  • The common natural options are fibre, fermented foods or extracts, and herbs used traditionally. Each has a different kind of evidence.[1][2]
  • Herbs can interact with medicines. Check with a doctor or pharmacist before starting.

What does "natural" mean for a gut health supplement?

There is no single definition. A product can call itself natural if it uses plant or fermented ingredients, yet still vary a great deal in quality, dose and evidence. Judge a product by its ingredient list and the evidence for it, not by the word on the front.

Which natural options are there?

Fibre

Fibre is food for the microbes already in your gut, and some fibres meet the scientific definition of a prebiotic.[1] Higher intakes of fibre from whole foods are linked with better health outcomes in large reviews of observational studies and trials.[3] Food comes first; a fibre supplement can top it up. Increase slowly, with water.

Fermented foods and extracts

The international consensus definition of fermented foods is foods made through desired microbial growth and conversion of food components.[2] That covers yoghurt, kimchi, tempeh and many more. A fermented herbal extract such as Herbaferm Drops is made by fermenting herbs, but it is a supplement, not a fermented food in the everyday sense.

Herbs

Chamomile, yarrow, elderflower, rosehip, propolis, oak bark and white wormwood each have a long history of traditional use in Europe. Traditional use is not the same as clinical evidence, and herbs can interact with medicines or be unsuitable in pregnancy. Our Ingredients page describes each herb as traditionally used, without health claims.

How do I use natural supplements safely?

  • Start with one product at a time so you can tell what is working or causing a reaction.
  • Read storage instructions. Products without preservatives, including Herbaferm Drops, are kept in the fridge.
  • Stop and speak to a professional if you have unusual or severe digestive symptoms.
  • Tell your doctor or pharmacist about any supplement if you take medication.

Frequently asked questions

Are natural supplements for gut health safer than other supplements?

Not automatically. Natural products can still cause side effects or interact with medicines. Check with a doctor or pharmacist.

Is fibre a natural gut health supplement?

Fibre feeds the microbes already in the gut and some fibres meet the definition of a prebiotic. Whole foods are the first source, and a supplement can add to them.
