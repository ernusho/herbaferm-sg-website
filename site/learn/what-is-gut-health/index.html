What is gut health? A plain-language explanation

Gut health is a popular phrase with a loose definition. Here is what it usually means and what is still being researched.

By Herbaferm editorial teamMedical reviewer: PendingPublished 2 Nov 2026 (draft)

In short
  • Gut health generally refers to the normal working of the digestive tract, including digestion, absorption, the gut barrier and the microbial community that lives there.[1]
  • The community is made of trillions of microbes. The best estimate is that human and bacterial cells in the body are about equal in number.[2]
  • There is no agreed test or score for gut health, and what a healthy mix looks like is still under study.[3]

What does gut health mean?

There is no single definition. A commonly cited one describes gut health as the absence of gastrointestinal complaints together with normal digestion and absorption, an effective barrier, a balanced microbiome and a well-functioning immune system in the gut.[1] The word is also used loosely in marketing, which is why it is worth asking what a source means by it.

What is the gut microbiome?

It is the community of microorganisms, mostly bacteria, that live in the digestive tract. A widely quoted older ratio of ten bacterial cells to every human cell was revised in 2016 to roughly one to one.[2] Everyone carries their own mix, and it changes with age, diet, medicines and where we live.[3]

Why do people talk about gut health so much?

Research links the microbiome with digestion, the immune system and metabolism, and interest has grown quickly.[3] Many of these findings are associations, and scientists are still working out which are causes. That is a reason for interest and for caution.

What are signs I should see a doctor?

Blood in the stool, black stool, persistent diarrhoea or constipation, unexplained weight loss, severe or ongoing pain, or trouble swallowing need medical assessment. Supplements are not a substitute.

What can I do for everyday gut health?

Start with food and routine: a variety of plant foods, fibre, fermented foods you enjoy, enough water, sleep and activity. Our guide on how to improve gut health goes into detail, and microbial dysbiosis explains what researchers mean by an imbalanced microbiome.

Frequently asked questions

What is gut health in simple terms?

It generally means that the digestive tract works normally, including digestion, absorption, the gut barrier and the community of microbes living there.

How many bacteria are in the gut?

Estimates are in the tens of trillions. A 2016 analysis found that bacterial and human cells in the body are roughly equal in number.
